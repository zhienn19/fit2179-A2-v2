Dengue Resurgence: A Nation Under the Shadow of Aedes

Urbanisation, climate patterns and population density fuel persistent outbreak

By The Health Desk | Graphics & Data Unit
As a tropical country, Malaysia has always been hospitable to mosquito populations. The combination of high humidity, frequent rainfall, and rapid urbanisation creates the ideal breeding ground for the Aedes vector. This turns a seasonal nuisance into an ongoing public health priority. This special data report examines a decade of epidemiological trends. It reveals sharp spikes in urban centres. There is also a worrying correlation between climate variables and case loads.

⚬ Since 2014 — Selangor accounts for nearly 35% of all reported cases.

⚬ Fatality risk — Elderly above 60 years face 4x higher mortality odds.

⚬ Rainfall correlation — Lagged 1 month precipitation shows strong outbreak linkage.

From the Editor's desk

Selangor and its surrounding urban centres consistently bear the highest reported confirmed cases of dengue fever. This is due to population density and rapid inter-district mobility. The Klang Valley remains the nation's epicentre of outbreaks.
